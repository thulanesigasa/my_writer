# 1. Biological Entities & Neurochemistry

## 1.1 Oxytocin
- **Category:** Neuropeptide / Hormone
- **Primary Site of Origin:** Paraventricular and supraoptic nuclei of the hypothalamus; released by the posterior pituitary.
- **Mechanisms & Function:** Functions as a social salience and bonding signal. In-group trust, maternal attachment, social recognition, anxiety reduction in safe social environments.
- **Critical Counter-Fact:** Oxytocin is NOT a simple "love hormone." It simultaneously increases out-group wariness, tribal defensiveness, and parochial altruism.

## 1.2 Cortisol & Glucocorticoids
- **Category:** Steroid Hormones (Adrenal Cortex)
- **Mechanisms & Function:** Primary stress hormone class operating via the Hypothalamic-Pituitary-Adrenal (HPA) axis. Mobilises glucose, heightens vigilance, suppresses non-essential systems (digestion, immune function) during threat.
- **Pathology:** Chronic elevated glucocorticoid exposure leads to hippocampal dendritic atrophy, anterior insula desensitisation, and persistent HPA axis hyper-reactivity.

## 1.3 Adrenaline (Epinephrine) & Norepinephrine
- **Category:** Catecholamines
- **Primary Site of Origin:** Adrenal medulla (adrenaline); Locus Coeruleus in the brainstem (norepinephrine).
- **Mechanisms & Function:** Rapid fight-or-flight activation, acute attentional focus, environmental salience tagging.

## 1.4 BDNF (Brain-Derived Neurotrophic Factor)
- **Category:** Neurotrophin / Growth Factor
- **Mechanisms & Function:** Molecular engine of neuroplasticity and adult neurogenesis (hippocampus). Promotes synaptic growth, dendritic spine density, and environmental adaptation. Stimulated by aerobic exercise, sleep, novelty, and psychological safety.

## 1.5 Dopamine & Serotonin
- **Dopamine:** Operates in mesolimbic/mesocortical pathways for reward prediction error, anticipation, and goal-directed motivation.
- **Serotonin:** Regulates mood, social hierarchy perception, impulse control, and gut-brain axis communication (90% synthesized in enterochromaffin cells of the GI tract).

# 2. Brain Regions & Functional Networks

## 2.1 vmPFC (Ventromedial Prefrontal Cortex)
- **Location:** Medial and ventral aspect of the prefrontal cortex.
- **Function:** Primary integration site for somatic markers. Connects subcortical emotional valuation with prefrontal decision options. Essential for intuitive risk assessment and personal value calculations.

## 2.2 Anterior Insula
- **Location:** Deep within the lateral sulcus of the cerebral cortex.
- **Function:** Cortical hub for interoception. Processes real-time bodily signals (heart rate, gut contraction, respiratory rhythm) and transforms them into subjective felt feelings ("gut feelings").

## 2.3 Default Mode Network (DMN)
- **Components:** Medial prefrontal cortex (mPFC), posterior cingulate cortex (PCC), precuneus, inferior parietal lobule.
- **Function:** Active during rest, mind-wandering, self-referential thought, autobiographical memory, theory of mind, and associative creative insight. Suppressed during task-focused executive attention.

## 2.4 dlPFC (Dorsolateral Prefrontal Cortex)
- **Location:** Lateral surface of the prefrontal cortex.
- **Function:** Executive control, working memory, explicit rule-following, rule-based analytical logic. Shows reduced activity (transient hypofrontality) during flow states.

## 2.5 Amygdala & HPA Axis
- **Amygdala:** Subcortical structure for threat salience detection, fear conditioning, and emotional memory tagging. Connects directly to the Hypothalamus to initiate the HPA axis cascade.

# 3. Core Theoretical Frameworks

## 3.1 Somatic Marker Hypothesis (Antonio Damasio)
- **Core Principle:** Emotion and bodily states guide decision-making. Past experiences leave somatic tags (markers) managed by the vmPFC and anterior insula. When facing choices, somatic markers rapidly filter options before conscious analysis begins.
- **Empirical Landmark:** Iowa Gambling Task (Bechara & Damasio, 1997).

## 3.2 Recognition-Primed Decision (RPD) Model (Gary Klein)
- **Core Principle:** Experts in high-stakes environments make decisions through rapid pattern recognition and serial mental simulation rather than parallel option comparison.
- **Empirical Landmark:** Naturalistic decision studies with firefighters, trauma surgeons, and military commanders.

## 3.3 Transient Hypofrontality & Flow (Arne Dietrich / Mihaly Csikszentmihalyi)
- **Core Principle:** Flow states occur when executive prefrontal monitoring (dlPFC) temporarily down-regulates, allowing implicit, highly trained pattern-recognition networks and DMN integration to run at full speed.
- **Empirical Landmark:** Jazz improvisation neuroimaging studies (Charles Limb, 2008).

## 3.4 Transgenerational Epigenetic Inheritance
- **Core Principle:** Environmental stress and trauma alter gene expression via DNA methylation and histone modification without altering underlying DNA sequences. Epigenetic marks on HPA-axis genes can be passed across generations.
- **Empirical Landmark:** Dutch Hunger Winter study (1944–45) and Holocaust survivor descendant studies.

## 3.5 Psychological Safety (Amy Edmondson)
- **Core Principle:** Team-level belief that interpersonal risk-taking is safe. Lowers amygdala threat responses and cortisol levels, enabling DMN engagement, open communication, and team innovation.
